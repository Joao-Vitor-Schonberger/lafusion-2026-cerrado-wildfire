\begin{abstract}
Wildfires in the Brazilian Cerrado represent a catastrophic environmental, ecological, and economic hazard, driven by prolonged seasonal droughts where relative humidity drops below 15\%. Operational early-warning systems remain hindered by two systemic shortcomings: reactive satellite detections with intrinsic observation latency, and unimodal meteorological alarms that produce severe false-positive rates by ignoring fuel combustibility. This study presents a rigorous Spatio-Temporal Information Fusion Framework and benchmark that integrates three heterogeneous data streams across a 10-year horizon (2016--2025; 222,650 aligned observations) over the State of Goi\'as and the Federal District: surface meteorological dynamics from automatic weather stations, land use and fuel flammability mappings, and antecedent satellite thermal memory. We systematically formulate and evaluate Early Feature-Level Fusion (Gradient Boosted Trees and Random Forests) against Late Decision-Level Fusion grounded in Dempster-Shafer Evidence Theory (DST) featuring dynamic spatial discounting. Evaluated under a strict Out-of-Time test protocol (2024--2025; 44,408 test samples) across multi-horizon proactive lead times (24h, 48h, and 72h), multimodal fusion substantially outperforms unimodal baselines. Early Fusion via LightGBM and XGBoost achieves peak overall balance (F1-score of 0.6774 and 0.6754; ROC-AUC of 0.9546), whereas Dempster-Shafer evidential fusion delivers maximum precision (73.33\% to 74.76\%), effectively suppressing operational false alarms. TreeSHAP interpretability isolates the compound non-linear triggers governing wildfire occurrence, proving that proactive fusion provides a reliable foundation for civil defense and environmental governance.

\keywords{Information Fusion \and Wildfire Risk Forecasting \and Dempster-Shafer Evidence Theory \and Multi-Horizon Prediction \and Brazilian Cerrado \and Explainable AI.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Brazilian Savanna (\textit{Cerrado}) is recognized as the world's most biodiverse tropical savanna, spanning over 2 million square kilometers. Despite its critical role in continental water recharge and global food security, the biome is increasingly vulnerable to severe wildfires during its prolonged dry season (June to October), when atmospheric relative humidity frequently plummets below 15\% and wind gusts reach critical thresholds~\cite{pivello2021cerrado}.

Operational monitoring systems currently deployed by civil protection and environmental defense agencies remain constrained by two fundamental trade-offs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Observation Latency of Satellite Detections:} Spaceborne sensors (e.g., MODIS, VIIRS) operate predominantly in a reactive capacity, registering thermal anomalies only after ignition and significant spatial propagation have already occurred~\cite{inpe2025}.
    \item \textbf{Alarmist False-Positive Rates in Meteorology-Only Models:} Systems based solely on atmospheric indices (e.g., Canadian FWI or Angstr\"om Index) produce excessive false alarms because they assume uniform flammability, failing to account for fuel moisture, vegetative biomass density, agricultural fire breaks, and spatial distance from ignition triggers~\cite{van2018fire}.
\end{enumerate}

To bridge this operational divide, this paper proposes an end-to-end \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Information Fusion Framework} that reconciles heterogeneous environmental streams to provide proactive (24h to 72h) wildfire risk forecasts across the State of Goi\'as and the Federal District. The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A Leakage-Free Spatio-Temporal Benchmark:} Construction and temporal alignment of a 10-year multi-source corpus (2016--2025, 222,650 grid observations at $0.10^\circ \approx 11\,\text{km}$ resolution), enforcing strict separation between past predictor features up to time $t$ and future active wildfire occurrences at $t+1$, $t+2$, and $t+3$.
    \item \textbf{Comparative Multimodal Fusion Paradigms:} Systematic empirical comparison of Early Feature-Level Fusion (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest) against Late Decision-Level Fusion utilizing \textbf{Dempster-Shafer Evidence Theory (DST)} equipped with dynamic spatial discounting, Weighted Soft-Voting, and Meta-Learner Stacking.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Horizon and Explainability Analysis:} Quantification of forecast degradation across 24h, 48h, and 72h lead times, complemented by TreeSHAP explainable AI (XAI) and cartographic risk validations on the out-of-time test set (2024--2025).
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Foundations of Evidential Information Fusion}
\label{sec:theory}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
According to the Joint Directors of Laboratories (JDL) data fusion model, information fusion can be conceptualized at multiple hierarchical tiers. While Level~1 (Early Fusion) concatenates raw or engineered features into a unified vector space, Level~2 (Late Decision Fusion) combines autonomous inferences from specialized domain classifiers, offering superior robustness in the presence of conflicting or missing sensor modalities~\cite{hall2001handbook}.

\subsection{Dempster-Shafer Evidence Theory}
Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST) provides a generalized mathematical framework for modeling epistemic uncertainty and belief under ignorance~\cite{shafer1976,dempster1967}. Let $\Omega = \{\text{Fire}, \neg\text{Fire}\}$ represent the binary frame of discernment. A Basic Belief Assignment (BBA), or mass function, maps the power set $2^\Omega \to [0, 1]$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
    m(\emptyset) = 0, \quad \sum_{A \subseteq \Omega} m(A) = 1
\end{equation}

For an individual sensor or classifier $i$ producing probability $p_i \in [0, 1]$ with an operational reliability factor $\alpha_i \in (0, 1]$, the evidentially discounted mass distribution is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    m_i(\{\text{Fire}\}) = \alpha_i \cdot p_i, \quad m_i(\{\neg\text{Fire}\}) = \alpha_i \cdot (1 - p_i), \quad m_i(\Omega) = 1 - \alpha_i
\end{equation}
where $m_i(\Omega)$ explicitly quantifies total epistemic ignorance.

\subsection{Orthogonal Combination and Decision Rule}
Given two independent evidence sources $m_1$ and $m_2$, Dempster's rule of combination yields the joint mass $m_{12}$:
\begin{equation}
    m_{12}(A) = \frac{1}{1 - K} \sum_{B \cap C = A} m_1(B) m_2(C), \quad \forall A \neq \emptyset
\end{equation}
where $K$ measures the degree of evidential conflict:
\begin{equation}
    K = m_1(\{\text{Fire}\}) m_2(\{\neg\text{Fire}\}) + m_1(\{\neg\text{Fire}\}) m_2(\{\text{Fire}\})
\end{equation}
Associativity allows recursive combination of arbitrary sources: $m_{123} = (m_1 \oplus m_2) \oplus m_3$. The fused decision probability is extracted via the Pignistic transformation~\cite{smets1994transferable}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{BetP}(\text{Fire}) = m_{123}(\{\text{Fire}\}) + \frac{1}{2} m_{123}(\Omega)
\end{equation}

\section{Proposed Spatio-Temporal Information Fusion Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Grid and Multi-Source Modalities}
The target geographic domain $\mathcal{G} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ encompassing Goi\'as and DF (latitudes $[-19.50^\circ, -12.30^\circ]$, longitudes $[-53.30^\circ, -45.90^\circ]$) is discretized into regular spatial cells $c_k \in \mathcal{C}$ ($\Delta \theta = 0.10^\circ \approx 11\,\text{km}$). The temporal corpus spans daily intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, 3{,}652\}$ across the 10-year period (2016--2025).

At each cell-day $(c_k, t)$, three heterogeneous modalities are aligned:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Meteorology $\mathbf{x}_{\text{weather}}(c_k, t)$:} Daily maximum and mean temperatures ($T_{\max}, T_{\text{mean}}$), minimum relative humidity ($H_{\min}$), wind gust velocity ($W_{\text{gust}}$), precipitation, consecutive dry days ($D_{\text{dry}}$), and rolling cumulative drought windows (3-day and 7-day averages).
    \item \textbf{Land Cover \& Combustible Biomass $\mathbf{x}_{\text{landuse}}(c_k)$:} Dominant vegetation class, flammability rating $I_{\text{flam}} \in [0.05, 0.90]$, fuel load $B_{\text{fuel}}$ (t/ha), distance to transport infrastructure $D_{\text{road}}$, and anthropic exposure.
    \item \textbf{Antecedent Satellite Memory $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sat\_lag}}(c_k, t)$:} Thermal detections strictly prior to time $t$ ($N_{\text{fire}}(t-1)$, $N_{\text{fire}}(t-2)$, 3-day sum, and previous maximum Fire Radiative Power $\text{FRP}_{\max}(t-1)$ in MW).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Index Formulation}
To incorporate thermodynamic fire spread principles into the feature space without inducing target circularity, we formulate the Fire Weather Index proxy:
\begin{equation}
    \text{FWI}_{\text{proxy}}(c_k, t) = \frac{T_{\max}(c_k, t) \cdot W_{\text{gust}}(c_k, t)}{H_{\min}(c_k, t) + 1.0}
\end{equation}
and the Fuel-Dryness Interaction Index:
\begin{equation}
    I_{\text{inter}}(c_k, t) = I_{\text{flam}}(c_k) \cdot \left(\frac{100.0 - H_{\min}(c_k, t)}{100.0}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamic Evidential Discounting in Dempster-Shafer Fusion}
Unlike conventional static reliability parameters, we formulate a \textbf{Dynamic Evidential Discounting} mechanism:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Weather Discounting:} The reliability of meteorological estimates decays exponentially as a function of the cell's distance $d_k$ to the nearest weather station:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha_w(c_k) = \max\left(0.50, \min\left(0.95, \, 0.92 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{d_k}{150\,\text{km}}\right)\right)\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Satellite Memory Discounting:} Antecedent thermal anomalies carry decaying evidential weight as the forecasting horizon $h \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ increases:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha_s(h) = \max\left(0.65, \, 0.90 \cdot \exp(-0.20 \cdot (h - 1))\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Fuel Flammability Reliability:} Constant baseline $\alpha_l = 0.82$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-Circular Multi-Horizon Target Definition}
A severe wildfire event is registered in cell $c_k$ on day $t$ if spaceborne sensors detect multiple concurrent fire pixels ($N_{\text{fire}} \ge 2$) or a high-intensity fire front ($\text{FRP}_{\max} \ge 35.0\,\text{MW}$). The multi-horizon predictive targets are strictly shifted forward:
\begin{equation}
    Y_{24h}(c_k, t) = \text{Event}(c_k, t+1), \quad Y_{48h}(c_k, t) = \text{Event}(c_k, t+2), \quad Y_{72h}(c_k, t) = \text{Event}(c_k, t+3)
\end{equation}
This guarantees zero temporal data leakage, as all input features belong to $\tau \le t$.

\section{Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Data Partitioning}
To mirror real-world operational deployment and prevent temporal autocorrelation leakage, we implement an \textbf{Out-of-Time split}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set (2016--2023):} 8 consecutive historical years comprising $178{,}242$ spatio-temporal observations ($15{,}872$ positive severe events; $8.90\%$).
    \item \textbf{Test Set (2024--2025):} 2 full unseen years comprising $44{,}408$ spatio-temporal observations ($4{,}213$ positive severe events; $9.49\%$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluated Models}
We evaluate nine model configurations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unimodal Baselines:} Weather-Only Random Forest, Fire-History-Only Random Forest, LandUse-Only Random Forest.
    \item \textbf{Early Feature-Level Fusion:} Random Forest (150 trees, max depth 14), XGBoost (150 estimators, max depth 6, lr 0.08), LightGBM (150 estimators, max depth 7, lr 0.08).
    \item \textbf{Late Decision-Level Fusion:} Dynamic Dempster-Shafer (DST), Weighted Soft-Voting ($0.45 p_w + 0.25 p_l + 0.30 p_s$), Meta-Learner Stacking (Logistic Regression meta-classifier).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis of Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Failure of Unimodal Baselines:} Weather-only models exhibit virtually no discriminative recall ($0.0596$) and poor precision ($0.3434$), confirming that meteorological dryness is an insufficient condition for ignition. Land-use alone fails completely (F1 = $0.0000$), demonstrating that static vegetative flammability cannot capture temporal dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Early Feature Fusion:} LightGBM and XGBoost achieve the highest overall predictive utility (F1 $\approx 0.677$, ROC-AUC $= 0.9546$). Joint feature spaces allow gradient boosted trees to uncover complex split conditions (e.g., high wind gust $\times$ high pasture biomass $\times$ low lagged humidity).
    \item \textbf{Evidential Conservatism of Dempster-Shafer:} Dempster-Shafer evidential fusion achieves the \textbf{highest precision among all models ($73.33\%$)}, outperforming XGBoost ($64.21\%$). By explicitly withholding commitment when sources conflict (represented by mass $m(\Omega)$), DST filters out spurious alarms, which is crucial for operational brigade deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This study developed a reproducible Spatio-Temporal Information Fusion Framework and evidential benchmark for multi-horizon wildfire risk forecasting in the Brazilian Cerrado, eliminating temporal data leakage and establishing rigorous baselines across a 10-year horizon (2016--2025). The experimental results demonstrate that multimodal fusion decisively overcomes the limitations of isolated sensors: Early Fusion delivers superior balanced classification (F1 $= 0.6774$, ROC-AUC $= 0.9546$), while Dempster-Shafer evidential fusion maximizes precision ($73.33\%$ to $74.76\%$), drastically curtailing false alarms. Future work will investigate continuous spatial deep evidential neural networks and near-real-time optical/SAR satellite embeddings.
